\subsection{Ablation Studies}

A progressive activation experiment isolates the contribution of each
component in FusionDet. Beginning with a stripped-down baseline that pairs
the CSP-Darknet backbone with a standard FPN, we reintroduce one module at a
time and retrain each variant for 300~epochs on COCO \texttt{train2017} using
the hyperparameters from Table~\ref{tab:hw_sw}. All configurations are scored
on \texttt{val2017}; outcomes appear in Table~\ref{tab:ablation}.

Baseline (CSP-Dark + FPN)           & 42.1 & 24.3 & 45.6 & 56.8 & 142 \\
+~Spatial Attention (SAM)           & 42.6 & 25.0 & 46.1 & 57.2 & 139 \\
+~Transformer Branch                & 44.3 & 27.4 & 47.9 & 58.1 & 118 \\
+~Adaptive Feature Fusion           & 45.1 & 28.6 & 48.7 & 58.5 & 114 \\

The ablation data reveal several instructive patterns. The Spatial Attention
Module provides a modest accuracy lift ($+0.5$~mAP) with almost no
throughput penalty (3~FPS), confirming it as a cost-efficient refinement
rather than a heavy computational block. The transformer branch proves to be
the single most impactful addition: the $+1.7$~mAP jump is skewed toward
small-object performance ($\text{AP}_{S}$ climbs by $2.4$ points), which
aligns with the expectation that non-local attention mechanisms are especially
useful when objects span only a handful of pixels and local convolutions
provide insufficient receptive field. The Adaptive Feature Fusion (AFF)
gate contributes $+0.8$~mAP over na\"ive channel concatenation at a cost of
4~FPS, illustrating that dynamically weighting the two branches according to
input context consistently outperforms a fixed merge strategy.

Replacing the vanilla FPN with the BiPANet neck delivers $+0.9$~mAP, and
the $\text{AP}_{S}$ gain of $1.2$~points specifically suggests that the
extra bottom-up pathway in BiPANet allows high-resolution spatial detail to
be propagated upward through the pyramid, enriching the deepest feature maps
with fine-grained cues that a top-down-only FPN discards. Channel and
cross-scale attention modules each add modest but consistent increments
($+0.5$ and $+0.4$~mAP, respectively) at negligible latency overhead.
Notably, switching from the conventional smooth-$L_1$ and binary-cross-entropy
pair to the CIoU-plus-Focal combination contributes a final $+0.5$~mAP
without altering the network topology, demonstrating that loss function
engineering remains a practical lever for extracting residual accuracy from a
converged architecture.

Taken together, the complete FusionDet model attains $47.4$~mAP at
$101$~FPS, representing a net accuracy gain of $5.3$~mAP over the baseline
at the expense of a $29\%$ reduction in throughput—a trade-off that positions
FusionDet on the Pareto frontier for the speed-versus-accuracy design space.

throughput figures are benchmarked on a single NVIDIA RTX~4090 GPU using
FP16 precision and a batch size of~1 to eliminate hardware variation as a
confounding factor.

FusionDet records a headline mAP of $47.4$, which clears YOLOv8-L
by $0.6$~points and RT-DETR-L by $1.4$~points while sustaining
$101$~FPS—within $7\%$ of the fastest competing method. The margin is widest
for small objects: FusionDet's $\text{AP}_{S}$ of $31.6$ outpaces YOLOv8-L
by $1.1$~points and RT-DETR-L by $3.5$~points, a difference attributable to
the complementary roles of the transformer's global receptive field and
BiPANet's bidirectional spatial propagation.

The two-stage Faster R-CNN achieves comparable headline accuracy ($42.0$~mAP)
but at fewer than one-quarter of FusionDet's frames per second. The
full-transformer DETR likewise fails to close the gap in either accuracy or
speed. SSD512 offers high throughput relative to its era but trails by more
than $17$~mAP, reflecting the ceiling imposed by anchor-based, non-hierarchical
detection without attention mechanisms. From a parameter-efficiency
standpoint, FusionDet's 38.2~M parameters sit between the compact
RT-DETR-L (32.0~M) and the heavier YOLOv5-L (46.5~M), while its
$118.4$~GFLOPs are broadly comparable to those of YOLOv7 (104.7~GFLOPs). This
confirms that the observed accuracy gains trace back to architectural
design choices—the dual-branch structure and multi-scale attention—rather than
to straightforward parameter budget expansion.
