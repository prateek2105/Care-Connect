\subsection{Ablation Studies}

To isolate the contribution of each architectural innovation,
we conduct a systematic ablation in which components are added
one at a time to a minimal baseline.  The baseline is a
standard FPN single-stage detector using the vanilla
MobileNetV3-Large backbone, a coupled (shared) detection head,
smooth-$L_1$ regression loss, and fixed IoU-threshold label
assignment.  All variants are trained under identical settings
(Table~\ref{tab:hw_sw}) for 300 epochs on \texttt{train2017}
and evaluated on \texttt{val2017}.

Table~\ref{tab:ablation} presents the component-wise ablation.
Each row introduces one additional element of FusionDet and
reports the resulting mAP, $\text{AP}_S$, and FPS.

The reconfigured backbone (B) contributes a $+2.0$~mAP gain
over the vanilla baseline while simultaneously reducing FPS
from 97 to 87, reflecting its larger effective receptive field.
The bidirectional neck (C) adds $+0.9$~mAP at a cost of
$5$~FPS, while the CBAM attention recalibration (D) provides
an additional $+1.7$~mAP improvement concentrated predominantly
in $\text{AP}_S$ ($+3.2$ points), confirming that spatial
attention is particularly beneficial for small objects.
Decoupling the detection head (E) contributes $+1.1$~mAP at
negligible FPS cost, validating the utility of resolving the
classification--regression feature conflict.  Replacing
smooth-$L_1$ with CIoU regression loss (F) yields $+0.8$~mAP
as expected from the richer gradient signal.  Focal loss (G)
adds $+0.5$~mAP by improving recall on hard foreground
examples, and SimOTA dynamic label assignment (H) delivers a
further $+1.2$~mAP by more accurately aligning positives with
ground-truth geometry in densely populated image regions.

Taken together, the full FusionDet model achieves
$50.3$~mAP---an absolute improvement of $8.2$~mAP over the
baseline---while the FPS reduction from 97 to 73 confirms that
the model operates comfortably in real-time scenarios.

\subsection{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We have presented FusionDet, a single-stage anchor-free
detector that jointly addresses the speed--precision trade-off
through a reconfigured efficient backbone, a bidirectional
attention-augmented feature pyramid neck, and a decoupled
detection head trained with CIoU and focal loss.  Systematic
ablation studies quantify the marginal contribution of each
component, and a comprehensive SOTA comparison demonstrates
that FusionDet achieves competitive accuracy on MS-COCO while
maintaining real-time throughput on a single commodity GPU.
Future work will explore knowledge distillation from larger
teacher networks, transformer-based attention in the neck, and
efficient deployment on edge hardware via structured pruning
and INT8 quantization.
