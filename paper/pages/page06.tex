\subsubsection{Bounding-Box Coordinate Encoding}

All box regression targets throughout FusionDet use the
center-size format.  Given a ground-truth box with top-left
corner $(x_1, y_1)$ and bottom-right corner $(x_2, y_2)$, the
canonical representation is:
\begin{equation}
    b = \left(
        \frac{x_1 + x_2}{2},\;
        \frac{y_1 + y_2}{2},\;
        x_2 - x_1,\;
        y_2 - y_1
    \right)
    = (x_c, y_c, w, h)
    \label{eq:bbox}
\end{equation}
Predictions at each feature-map location $(i, j)$ of level
$l$ are decoded as offsets from the stride-aligned grid point
$\left(i \cdot s_l,\; j \cdot s_l\right)$, where $s_l \in
\{8, 16, 32, 64\}$ is the stride of pyramid level $l$.

\subsubsection{Intersection over Union}

The Intersection over Union (IoU) between a predicted box
$b_p = (x_c^p, y_c^p, w^p, h^p)$ and a ground-truth box
$b_{gt}$ is:
\begin{equation}
    \text{IoU}(b_p, b_{gt}) =
    \frac{\text{Area}(b_p \cap b_{gt})}
         {\text{Area}(b_p \cup b_{gt})}
    \label{eq:iou}
\end{equation}
IoU is scale-invariant (a small box must achieve the same
proportional overlap as a large box to receive the same score),
making it more robust than pixel-area-based alternatives as a
matching criterion and as a component of loss functions.

\subsection{Dynamic Label Assignment: SimOTA}

A critical design decision in anchor-free detectors is how to
assign ground-truth objects to prediction locations during
training.  Static rules---such as assigning any anchor with
IoU above a threshold to the corresponding ground-truth box---
are sensitive to threshold choice and fail gracefully near
boundaries.  FusionDet adopts Simplified Optimal Transport
Assignment (SimOTA)~\cite{ge2021yolox}, which formulates label
assignment as an optimal transport problem solved efficiently
with a greedy matching algorithm.

\textbf{Cost matrix construction.}  For each ground-truth box
$g_j$, a set of $k$ candidate prediction locations is first
identified as those falling within a dynamic center region
whose size scales with the object's predicted box width and
height.  For each candidate $(i,j)$ pair, the assignment cost
is:
\begin{equation}
    c_{ij} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{cls},ij}
            + \lambda_{\text{geo}}\,\mathcal{L}_{\text{CIoU},ij}
    \label{eq:simota_cost}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{L}_{\text{cls},ij}$ is the focal loss between
the current predicted class distribution and the ground-truth
label, and $\mathcal{L}_{\text{CIoU},ij}$ measures the
geometric alignment between the predicted box and the
ground-truth box.  The coefficient $\lambda_{\text{geo}} = 3.0$
follows~\cite{ge2021yolox}.

\textbf{Dynamic top-$k$ selection.}  The number of positive
locations allocated to each ground-truth box is determined
dynamically as:
\begin{equation}
    k_j = \left\lfloor
        \sum_{i \in \mathcal{T}_j}
        \text{IoU}(b_p^{(i)}, g_j)
    \right\rfloor
    \label{eq:topk}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{T}_j$ is the set of the top-10 candidate
locations (by IoU) for ground-truth box $g_j$.  The floor
ensures $k_j \geq 1$, guaranteeing at least one positive for
every ground-truth instance.  This data-driven allocation
naturally assigns more positives to large, easily detected
objects and fewer positives to small or heavily occluded ones,
producing a more calibrated training signal than fixed-$k$
alternatives.

\textbf{Conflict resolution.}  When a prediction location is
selected as a positive for multiple ground-truth boxes---a
frequent occurrence in crowded scenes---it is exclusively
assigned to the ground-truth box for which the assignment cost
$c_{ij}$ is lowest, and the other assignments are discarded.

SimOTA improves mAP by approximately $1.2$ points over a fixed
IoU-threshold assignment strategy at negligible computational
cost during training, as confirmed by our ablation in
Table~\ref{tab:assignment_ablation}.

SimOTA's advantage is most pronounced on small-object categories,
where the pool of viable positive locations is small and
precise geometric calibration of the cost matrix is most
critical.  Its dynamic $k$ selection adapts naturally to object
density: in crowded scenes it tends to assign fewer positives
per object to reduce cross-object interference, while in sparse
scenes it increases the positive count to stabilize gradient
variance.

\subsubsection{Neck Parameter Efficiency}

The complete neck---bidirectional fusion with CBAM
recalibration---introduces $2.1$~million additional
parameters over the backbone (Table~\ref{tab:neck_ablation}).
This is approximately $30\%$ of the total FusionDet parameter
budget, confirming that the neck is the dominant contributor
to both accuracy gain and additional cost.  Given that the
$2.6$~mAP improvement over the FPN baseline is achieved with
$2.1$~M parameters and an $11$~FPS reduction, the neck
contributes approximately $0.24$~mAP per million neck
parameters and $0.24$~mAP per FPS unit lost---a favorable
accuracy-efficiency trade-off compared with backbone scaling,
which delivers approximately $0.15$~mAP per million parameters
at comparable model sizes.
